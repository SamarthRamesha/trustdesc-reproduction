# Statistical analysis

All numbers come from `reports/recovered_descgen/run_state.json` and
`reports/dynver_recovered/results.json`, regenerated by
`scripts/analyze_statistics.py` into `STATISTICAL_RESULTS.json` / `.csv`.
Nothing here is the paper's task success rate: the paper's 208-task benchmark,
its labels and its traces were never released, so no comparable metric exists.

## 1. What the samples are

| Stage | Unit of analysis | n | Independence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corpus | tool slot | 30 (Python) + 22 (JS/TS, not sliced) | each slot is one source entry point |
| DescGen | generation attempt | 28 | one attempt per tool; two attempts per tool when the first was rejected |
| DynVer | executed claim | 18 | **not independent**: 6 claims come from 3 tools, and each tool contributes 6 units |

The DynVer unit count is the single most important caveat. Eighteen units sound
like eighteen observations, but they are six claims per condition drawn from three
tools. Effective independent replication is closer to *three tools* than to eighteen.

## 2. Description generation yield

**10 valid descriptions from 28 attempts = 35.7%** (Wilson 95% CI 20.7%–54.2%).

| Rejection category | n | share of rejections |
|---|---|---|
| Generation deadline (90 s, partial reply rejected) | 8 | 44.4% |
| Identifier normalisation rejected | 7 | 38.9% |
| Input over the 4096-token cap | 2 | 11.1% |
| Schema / argument-name validation | 1 | 5.6% |
| **Total rejected** | **18** | 100% |

Two findings matter more than the headline yield:

1. **The dominant failure is time, not comprehension.** Eight of eighteen rejections
   are the 90-second deadline. The model was usually mid-answer, and the partial reply
   was discarded rather than truncated into an invalid description. Raising the cap is
   the cheapest single improvement available.
2. **Identifier normalisation is a tooling defect, not a model defect.** Seven
   rejections come from our own rule that rejects over-long or invalid identifiers.
   That is a fixable validator, and it accounts for a quarter of all failures.

Timing (seconds, per attempt):

| | n | median | mean | sd | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Valid generations | 10 | 133.8 | 131.7 | 47.0 | 1317.2 |
| Rejected attempts | 18 | 137.6 | 114.5 | 74.1 | 2061.2 |
| **All attempts** | 28 | — | — | — | **3378.3 (≈56 min)** |

Valid descriptions carried 6–11 claims each (median 8), **88 claims in total**.
Only 18 of those 88 claims were ever tested, because only one server had an
adapter shim layer at the time of the run.

## 3. DynVer verdicts

**12 supported, 6 refuted, 0 inconclusive of 18 units = 66.7% supported**
(Wilson 95% CI 43.8%–83.7%).

| Condition | Supported | Refuted | Inconclusive | Rate | Wilson 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original (developer text) | 4 | 2 | 0 | 66.7% | 30.0%–90.3% |
| full (TRUSTDESC) | 4 | 2 | 0 | 66.7% | 30.0%–90.3% |
| lite (TRUSTDESC, reduced claims) | 4 | 2 | 0 | 66.7% | 30.0%–90.3% |

By tool, pooling conditions:

| Tool | Supported | Refuted | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| `watch_topic` | 5 / 6 | 1 / 6 | bookkeeping behaviour matches descriptions |
| `list_watches` | 5 / 6 | 1 / 6 | one claim about returned state was wrong |
| `unwatch_topic` | 2 / 6 | 4 / 6 | descriptions promise deletion semantics the tool does not deliver in a fresh state |

The `unwatch_topic` result is the one genuinely interesting signal in the pilot: four
refutations concentrated on a single tool, each caused by the tool reporting
`Watch not found` where the description implied success. That is exactly the kind of
description/behaviour gap TRUSTDESC is meant to catch — but with four units on one
tool it is an observation, not a measured effect.

Execution cost: median 11.2 s per unit (mean 11.5, sd 4.6, max 21.5 s), 206.5 s total
for all 18 units. Every unit exited normally (`exited`, returncode 0) and returned an
`ok` observation, so no verdict is an artefact of a crashed process or a timeout.

## 4. Cross-condition comparison

The three conditions produced **identical aggregate counts**. Two readings must be
kept apart:

- **Aggregate counts are equal.** 4/2 in every condition.
- **The tasks were not the same.** Each condition generated its own tasks from its own
  description, so task index 0 in `original` is a different task from task index 0 in
  `full`. `STATISTICAL_RESULTS.json` records `tasks_identical_across_conditions: false`.

Comparing verdicts cell-by-cell *by index* therefore measures nothing causal; it only
says how often two different task sets happened to land on the same verdict:

| Pair | Identical verdicts | Cells |
|---|---|---|
| original vs full | 4 | 6 |
| original vs lite | 4 | 6 |
| full vs lite | 4 | 6 |

## 5. What we deliberately did not compute

| Method | Why not |
|---|---|
| McNemar / paired test | The cells are not the same tasks across conditions, so pairing is invalid. |
| Chi-square / Fisher on 3×2 | Expected counts below 5 in most cells; n = 18 with 6 per condition. |
| Equivalence test (TOST) | Identical counts are not equivalence. With n = 6, the CI spans 30%–90%; a true difference of 20 points is entirely compatible with these data. |
| Inter-rater agreement (kappa) | Kappa is undefined when both raters use a single category in the paired cells; reporting it would be misleading. |
| Any p-value on the three-condition split | No randomisation, no independent sampling, and the same three tools underlie every condition. |

**No significance test is reported because none would be meaningful at this sample size.**
That is a deliberate analytical choice, not an omission.

Power: detecting a 20-percentage-point difference between two conditions at 80% power
(α = 0.05, two-sided) needs roughly **90 independent units per condition**. The pilot has
6 per condition, from 3 tools.

## 6. What the null result means

Three interpretations are available, and the data cannot separate them:

1. **The descriptions are equally good.** All three describe the same three small tools
   accurately enough that behaviour agrees at the same rate.
2. **The task generator is the bottleneck.** Tasks came from a 4B model at temperature
   0.2; two tasks per tool is too coarse to expose differences between descriptions.
3. **The tool slice is the bottleneck.** Three bookkeeping tools in one server share most
   of their behaviour, so any description of them is largely correct.

Interpretation 3 is the most likely contributor given `unwatch_topic` carries all four
refutations, and interpretation 1 cannot be concluded from identical counts.

## 7. What would be needed to conclude anything

- **Scale**: ≥ 90 units per condition, drawn from ≥ 10 tools across ≥ 4 servers.
- **Paired design**: generate *one* task set per tool, then run every condition on the
  same tasks, so a paired test is legitimate.
- **Blinding**: judge without revealing which condition produced the description.
- **Second judge**: a different model family, with disagreement reported.
- **Human spot-check**: at least 20 verdicts re-judged by a person.
- **Fixed validator**: repair identifier normalisation and raise the token cap, then
  re-measure yield on the same 28 slices.
- **Original material**: the paper's tasks and labels, which remain unavailable.

## 8. Bottom line

We measured a 35.7% description yield and a 66.7% claim-support rate across 18
sandboxed executions. The three description conditions did not separate. The sample is
too small and too clustered for inferential claims, the tasks differ across conditions
by construction, and the paper's own benchmark and metrics remain unreproduced. The
honest summary is a bounded pilot with one suggestive tool-level signal, not a
replication.
